## The Chained Lady

Andromeda is a long chain of stars running out from the corner of the Great Square of
Pegasus, representing a princess bound to a rock at the sea's edge. It is also the home of
the Andromeda Galaxy — the most distant object most people will ever see with the naked
eye.

## Mythology

Andromeda was the daughter of King Cepheus and Queen Cassiopeia of Ethiopia. Cassiopeia
boasted that her daughter was more beautiful than the Nereids, the sea nymphs, and the
offended Poseidon sent the sea monster Cetus to ravage the coast. An oracle told Cepheus
that the only way to appease the god was to sacrifice his daughter, so Andromeda was
chained to a rock by the shore to await the monster.

She was rescued by Perseus, who was flying home after killing the gorgon Medusa. He turned
Cetus to stone — or cut it apart, depending on the telling — freed Andromeda, and married
her. Nearly the whole cast was eventually placed in the sky together: Andromeda beside her
rescuer Perseus, with her parents Cepheus and Cassiopeia above and the monster Cetus
lurking below.

## Notable stars and objects

- **Alpheratz** — the princess's head, a hot blue-white star about 97 light-years away. It
  doubles as the northeastern corner of the Great Square of Pegasus, shared between the two
  constellations.
- **Mirach** — a cool orange giant roughly 200 light-years away, marking Andromeda's hip
  and serving as the signpost to the galaxy.
- **Almach** — about 355 light-years away and one of the finest coloured doubles in the
  sky: a golden giant with a bluish companion, easily split in a small telescope.
- **The Andromeda Galaxy (M31)** — a spiral galaxy of a trillion stars and the nearest
  large galaxy to our own. At magnitude 3.4 it is visible to the unaided eye from a dark
  site as a faint elongated smudge.
- **M32 and M110** — two small satellite galaxies flanking M31, at magnitudes 8.1 and 8.5.
  Both need binoculars or a telescope.

## How to find it

Look high in the southern sky on a November evening from the Northern Hemisphere. Start
with the Great Square of Pegasus and find its northeastern corner, Alpheratz; from there
two chains of stars trail northeast toward the W of Cassiopeia, passing Mirach and Almach.

To find the galaxy, go to Mirach and step two stars north — the second faint smudge is M31
itself. Use averted vision: look slightly to one side of it, and it brightens noticeably.
