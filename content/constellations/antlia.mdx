## The Air Pump

Antlia is a small, dim constellation in the southern sky, with no star brighter than
fourth magnitude and no bright pattern to catch the eye. It honours not a hero or a beast
but a piece of laboratory equipment: the air pump.

## History

Antlia was invented in the 1750s by the French astronomer Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille, who
spent two years at the Cape of Good Hope cataloguing the southern stars. Finding wide
stretches of faint sky that the ancients had never named, he filled them with the
instruments of the Enlightenment — a telescope, a clock, a furnace, a sculptor's studio,
and this one, the air pump.

The pump he had in mind was the one invented by the French physicist Denis Papin for his
experiments on air and the vacuum. Lacaille first called it *la Machine Pneumatique*; he
later Latinised the name to Antlia Pneumatica, and it was finally shortened to plain
Antlia. There is no myth attached to these stars, and no ancient figure beneath them —
they are simply a modern monument to a laboratory device.

## Notable stars

- **Alpha Antliae** — the brightest star of the constellation at magnitude 4.25, an orange
  giant about 367 light-years away. Even so, it is barely visible from a city.
- **Epsilon Antliae** — another orange giant, magnitude 4.51, and far more distant at some
  700 light-years.
- **Iota Antliae** — magnitude 4.60 and the nearest of the group at about 199 light-years.
- **Theta Antliae** — a white star of magnitude 4.79, roughly 384 light-years away.

## How to find it

Antlia is best placed on April evenings, and strongly favours the Southern Hemisphere,
where it rides high overhead in the autumn sky. Northern observers can catch it low in the
south, but it needs a clear horizon and a dark night.

Look for it between Hydra's long winding body to the north and Vela to the south, with the
faint sprawl of Pyxis to the west. There is no distinctive shape to recognise — Antlia is
a quiet triangle of faint stars, best found by working out from its brighter neighbours
and knowing that the empty patch you have arrived at is the one you were looking for.
