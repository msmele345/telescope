## The Eagle

Aquila is the eagle of Zeus, flying up the summer Milky Way with the brilliant white star
Altair at its neck. It straddles the celestial equator, which means almost everyone on
Earth gets a good view of it.

## Mythology

In Greek myth the eagle belonged to Zeus — the bird that carried his thunderbolts and ran
his errands across the sky. Its most famous errand was the abduction of Ganymede, a
beautiful Trojan prince whom Zeus coveted as cupbearer to the gods. The eagle snatched the
boy from the slopes of Mount Ida and carried him to Olympus, and the two were set in the
sky side by side: Aquila the eagle, and Aquarius, the water-bearer, just below.

A darker story attaches to the same bird. It was an eagle that Zeus sent each day to tear
at the liver of Prometheus, chained to his rock for giving fire to humankind — until
Heracles shot it down.

Chinese astronomy tells a gentler tale of these stars. Altair is the Herdboy, separated
from his love the Weaver Girl (Vega) by the great river of the Milky Way, and allowed to
cross it to meet her only one night each year.

## Notable stars

- **Altair** — at magnitude 0.77 one of the brightest stars in the sky, and one of the
  nearest bright stars at just 17 light-years. It spins so fast that it is visibly
  flattened into an oval rather than a sphere.
- **Tarazed** — the orange giant just above Altair, about 460 light-years away. It looks
  like Altair's companion but lies far beyond it.
- **Alshain** — the fainter star just below Altair, about 45 light-years away. With
  Tarazed it forms the short, straight line of three that makes Aquila easy to recognise.
- **Deneb el Okab** — "the tail of the eagle," a hot blue-white star some 83 light-years
  distant, marking the far end of the bird from Altair.

## How to find it

Look high in the south on an August evening from the Northern Hemisphere, or high in the
north in the Southern Hemisphere's winter. Altair is one corner of the Summer Triangle,
the huge asterism it forms with Vega and Deneb — Altair is the southernmost and the one
flanked closely by two fainter stars, one above and one below.

That little row of three is the surest marker in this part of the sky. From it, the
eagle's wings spread out to either side along the glowing band of the Milky Way.
