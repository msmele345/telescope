## The Altar

Ara is a small, compact constellation low in the southern sky — an altar whose smoke, in
the old telling, rises as the band of the Milky Way. It carries no bright marquee star,
but its figure is tidy and easy to trace once you have found Scorpius above it.

## Mythology

The Greeks knew these stars as the altar on which the Olympian gods swore their alliance
before making war on the Titans. Zeus and his siblings burned offerings here and pledged
themselves to one another, and after the victory the altar was set in the heavens as a
memorial of the oath that won the world.

Because the Milky Way runs thick through this part of the sky, the ancients read the
glowing band as the smoke drifting up from the altar fire. Some versions make the altar
the one built by the centaur Chiron, or the one on which Noah's sacrifice was offered in
later Christian retellings — but the oath of the gods is the story that stuck.

## Notable stars

- **Beta Arae** — the brightest star of the altar, an orange supergiant some 600
  light-years away, shining at magnitude 2.85.
- **Alpha Arae** — a fierce blue star at magnitude 2.95, about 242 light-years off and
  hot enough, at some 26,000 K, to glow distinctly blue-white beside its orange neighbour.
- **Gamma Arae** — a blue supergiant over a thousand light-years distant, magnitude 3.34,
  and one of the more luminous stars of the southern Milky Way.
- **Cervantes** — far too faint to see unaided at magnitude 5.15, but notable as a nearby
  Sun-like star only 50 light-years away, with a family of planets named for characters
  from *Don Quixote*.

## How to find it

Ara is a Southern Hemisphere constellation, best placed on winter evenings there — July is
ideal — when it rides high in the south. From mid-northern latitudes it barely clears the
horizon, if at all.

Start from Scorpius and follow the curve of its tail down to the stinger. Just below and
south of it lies Ara, a stubby rectangle of stars with Beta and Alpha side by side at its
heart. The bright triangle of Alpha Centauri and Beta Centauri lies off to the west, and
the Milky Way pours straight through the whole scene.
