## The Ram

Aries is the first sign of the zodiac and one of the more modest constellations to look
at — a small, bent line of stars that takes some imagination to see as a ram.

## Mythology

In Greek myth, Aries is the Golden Ram. The story begins with two royal children, Phrixus
and Helle, whose stepmother plotted to have them sacrificed. The god Hermes sent a winged
ram with a fleece of pure gold to carry them to safety. Helle slipped from its back over
the strait that still bears her name — the Hellespont — but Phrixus reached the kingdom of
Colchis. There the ram was sacrificed and its golden fleece hung in a sacred grove,
guarded by a sleepless dragon. Generations later, Jason and the Argonauts would set out to
steal it.

In Babylonian astronomy, the same stars were "the Hired Man," and earlier still they were
associated with a farm laborer or a sacrificial worker. The ram identification became
fixed by the time of the Greeks and has stayed that way for over two thousand years.

## Notable stars

- **Hamal** — the brightest star, an orange giant about 66 light-years away. Its name
  comes from Arabic for "head of the ram."
- **Sheratan** — a blue-white star marking another point of the ram's head.
- **Mesarthim** — a notable double star that early telescopic observers in the 1600s used
  to demonstrate that some stars come in pairs.

## How to find it

Aries sits east of Pisces and west of Taurus. The trick is to find the bright Pleiades
star cluster (in Taurus) and then look back to the west — Aries is the small group of
stars between the Pleiades and the Great Square of Pegasus. It is at its highest in the
evening sky from late autumn through early winter.
