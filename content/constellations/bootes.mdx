## The Herdsman

Boötes is a tall kite- or ice-cream-cone-shaped figure in the late spring and summer sky,
anchored by Arcturus — one of the brightest stars in the whole heavens. The name is Greek
for "ox-driver," and the constellation has been herding the stars of the Big Dipper around
the pole for as long as anyone has been naming them.

## Mythology

The Greeks could not agree on who Boötes was, and told several stories at once. In one, he
is Arcas, the son of Callisto, placed in the sky beside his mother after Zeus turned her
into the Great Bear — which explains the name of his brightest star, Arcturus, "the guardian
of the bear." Night after night he follows Ursa Major around the pole, never catching her.

In another telling he is Icarius, an Athenian farmer taught the art of winemaking by
Dionysus. He shared his wine with his neighbours, who drank it, felt strange, assumed they
had been poisoned, and killed him. Dionysus set him among the stars in grief.

A third tradition makes him simply a ploughman, driving the oxen of the Big Dipper — in
some older sky maps, the seven stars of the Dipper are his plough and his team, and Boötes
walks behind them with a pair of hunting dogs (the small constellation Canes Venatici) at
his heel.

## Notable stars

- **Arcturus** — an orange giant and the brightest star north of the celestial equator, at
  magnitude −0.04. It lies just 37 light-years away, and its cooler surface — around
  4850 K — gives it a distinctly warm, butterscotch colour to the naked eye.
- **Izar** — one of the loveliest double stars in the sky. A small telescope splits it into
  a magnitude 2.7 golden giant and a fainter blue companion near magnitude 5.1, about 200
  light-years away. Its nickname is *Pulcherrima*, "the most beautiful."
- **Muphrid** — a yellow-white star of magnitude 2.7, close to Arcturus in the sky and, at
  37 light-years, genuinely its near neighbour in space as well.
- **Seginus** — magnitude 3.0, marking the herdsman's left shoulder, about 85 light-years
  away.
- **Nekkar** — magnitude 3.5 at the top of the figure, the herdsman's head.

## How to find it

Start with the Big Dipper and follow the curve of its handle away from the bowl, continuing
the arc outward — the old mnemonic is "arc to Arcturus." The first brilliant star you reach
is Arcturus, and from there the rest of Boötes rises above it as a long, narrow kite, with
Nekkar at the point and Izar and Seginus forming the shoulders.

Look high in the southern sky on a June evening from the Northern Hemisphere; Arcturus will
be hard to miss.
