## The Greater Dog

Canis Major is the larger of Orion's two hunting dogs, following at his heels across the
winter sky. It holds Sirius, the brightest star in the night — a blue-white beacon that
outshines everything except the planets and the Moon.

## Mythology

To the Greeks, Canis Major was the faithful hound of Orion, forever chasing the hare
Lepus across the sky just beneath the Hunter's feet. Some versions name the dog Laelaps,
a magical hound destined always to catch its prey, set loose after a fox that was destined
never to be caught. Faced with a paradox he could not resolve, Zeus turned both animals to
stone and lifted the dog into the heavens.

Sirius itself carried a reputation of its own. The Greeks called it the "scorching one,"
and its reappearance in the dawn sky in late summer marked the hottest stretch of the
year — the "dog days." In Egypt the same rising announced the annual flooding of the Nile,
and the calendar was built around it.

## Notable stars

- **Sirius** — the Dog Star, and the brightest star in the night sky at magnitude −1.46.
  Much of that brilliance comes from sheer proximity: it lies just 9 light-years away, one
  of the Sun's nearest neighbours. Low on the horizon it flashes red, white and blue as
  the atmosphere splits its light.
- **Adhara** — the dog's hind leg, a fiercely hot blue star about 430 light-years off. It
  is one of the strongest sources of ultraviolet light in the whole sky.
- **Wezen** — a yellow-white supergiant roughly 1,800 light-years away. It looks modest at
  magnitude 1.84 only because of that enormous distance.
- **Mirzam** — "the announcer," so named because it rises just before Sirius and warns of
  its arrival.
- **Aludra** — the faintest of the main figure at magnitude 2.45, and the most remote, some
  3,200 light-years from us.

## How to find it

Look southeast on a February evening — high overhead from the Southern Hemisphere, lower
down from the north. The easiest route is through Orion: follow the three stars of the
Belt down and to the left, and the line runs straight into Sirius.

From Sirius, the body of the dog hangs below and to the south, a rough triangle of Adhara,
Wezen and Aludra marking its hindquarters and tail.
