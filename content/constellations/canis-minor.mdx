## The Little Dog

Canis Minor is a tiny constellation of just two noticeable stars — but one of them,
Procyon, is among the brightest in the whole sky. It trots along behind Orion as the
smaller of the hunter's two dogs.

## Mythology

The Greeks saw in these stars one of Orion's hunting hounds, following at the heels of
Canis Major as the hunter strides across the winter sky. The name Procyon means "before
the dog" — it rises shortly ahead of Sirius, the Dog Star, announcing its arrival.

Another story attaches the constellation to Maera, the dog of Icarius. When Icarius was
given wine by Dionysus and shared it with shepherds who, drunk for the first time,
believed they had been poisoned and killed him, Maera led his daughter Erigone to the
body. All three were placed in the sky in memory of the tragedy.

## Notable stars

- **Procyon** — the eighth-brightest star in the night sky at magnitude 0.38, and one of
  our closest stellar neighbours at just 11 light-years. It is a yellow-white star a
  little hotter than the Sun, with a faint white dwarf companion orbiting it.
- **Gomeisa** — the only other star of the pair easy to see, at magnitude 2.9 and about
  170 light-years away. It is a fiercely hot blue star, far more luminous than Procyon
  but much more distant.
- **Gamma Canis Minoris** — an orange star of magnitude 4.3, some 400 light-years off.
  You will need a dark sky to pick it out.

## How to find it

Look for Orion on a February evening, then find the three stars of his belt and follow
them down and to the left to brilliant Sirius. Procyon lies up and to the left of Sirius,
completing the large equilateral triangle those two make with the red Betelgeuse — an
asterism known as the Winter Triangle.

Canis Minor sits close to the celestial equator, so it is visible from both hemispheres.
Having found Procyon, look a short way to the northwest for Gomeisa. That is very nearly
the whole dog.
