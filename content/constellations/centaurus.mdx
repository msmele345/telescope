## The Centaur

Centaurus is one of the great constellations of the southern sky — a half-man, half-horse
figure sprawling across the Milky Way. It holds the closest star system to our own, and
two of the brightest stars in the entire sky.

## Mythology

The Greeks usually identified this figure with Chiron, the wisest of the centaurs. Unlike
his wild and violent kin, Chiron was a healer and a teacher, tutor to Achilles, Jason and
Asclepius. He was struck by accident with an arrow poisoned by the blood of the Hydra —
and being immortal, he could not die of the wound, only suffer it. He gave up his
immortality to end the pain, and Zeus set him among the stars in honour of his learning.

Some tellings give the constellation instead to Pholus, another gentle centaur, killed by
the same poisoned arrows. The two bright stars at the figure's feet carry Arabic
names — Rigil Kentaurus means "foot of the centaur," and Hadar is simply "the ground."
Centaurus is an ancient grouping, listed by Ptolemy in the second century.

## Notable stars

- **Rigil Kentaurus** — the brightest star of Alpha Centauri, at magnitude −0.01 on its own,
  and with its companion the third-brightest star system in the night sky. At just over 4
  light-years away it is part of the nearest star system to the Sun, and a close twin of the
  Sun in colour and temperature.
- **Alpha² Centauri** — Rigil Kentaurus's companion, a slightly cooler orange star of
  magnitude 1.33. A small telescope splits the pair cleanly.
- **Hadar** — the centaur's other foot, magnitude 0.61. It looks almost as bright as its
  neighbour, but it is a fiercely hot blue giant some 525 light-years away.
- **Menkent** — "shoulder of the centaur," a yellow star about 61 light-years from us,
  marking the figure's upper body well to the north of the two bright feet.
- **Gamma Centauri** — a white star of magnitude 2.17 about 130 light-years away, sitting
  near the centaur's torso.
- **Epsilon Centauri** — another blue giant, magnitude 2.30, one of several hot stars
  tracing the centaur's body through the Milky Way.

## How to find it

Centaurus is a Southern Hemisphere constellation, best seen on evenings around May, when
it rides high overhead from southern latitudes. Look first for Crux, the Southern Cross;
Centaurus wraps around it, and Rigil Kentaurus and Hadar lie just to the east of the Cross
as a close, brilliant pair of stars.

Those two are the famous "pointers" that lead back to Crux and help southern observers
find true south. From there the fainter stars of the centaur's body curve up and north
toward Menkent. Observers in the mid-northern latitudes never see the brightest part of
this constellation at all — it stays below the horizon.
