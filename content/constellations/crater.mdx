## The Cup

Crater is a small, faint pattern of modest stars south of Leo — a goblet tipped on its side
in the spring sky. Nothing in it is bright, but its shape is a tidy little bowl once your
eyes adjust, and it comes with one of the better myths in the sky.

## Mythology

Crater is the cup of Apollo, and its story belongs to its neighbour Corvus the crow. Apollo
sent the crow — then a bird with silver-white feathers — to fetch water in this cup. On the
way the crow found a fig tree with unripe fruit and waited several days for the figs to
ripen, forgetting his errand entirely.

Returning late, the crow invented an excuse: a water snake, he said, had blocked the
spring. Apollo saw through the lie at once, scorched the bird's feathers black, and flung
all three into the sky — the crow, the cup, and the water snake Hydra. There the crow sits
forever beside the cup of water he cannot reach, with Hydra coiled below to keep him from
it.

## Notable stars

- **Delta Crateris** — the constellation's brightest star at magnitude 3.6, a yellowish
  giant about 195 light-years away. It marks the base of the bowl.
- **Alkes** — "the cup" in Arabic, and the star that gives the constellation its name.
  At magnitude 4.1 it lies about 174 light-years off.
- **Gamma Crateris** — equal in brightness to Alkes but much nearer, around 84
  light-years, and noticeably hotter and whiter.
- **Beta Crateris** — a hot blue-white star of magnitude 4.5, some 266 light-years away,
  sitting at the foot of the goblet.

## How to find it

Crater is best seen on April evenings, and it sits high and comfortable for Southern
Hemisphere observers while northern ones must look low toward the south.

Start from Leo. Drop south from Denebola, the star at the lion's tail, and you will come to
a lopsided quadrilateral of four stars — that is Corvus the crow. Crater lies immediately
to the right (west) of Corvus, between it and the bright Spica's long line of Hydra below.
Under city lights the cup may vanish entirely; give it a dark sky and let your eyes settle.
