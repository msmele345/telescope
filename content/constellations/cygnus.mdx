## The Swan

Cygnus flies down the length of the Milky Way with its wings spread wide and its long neck
stretched toward the southern horizon. Its brightest stars also form one of the sky's
clearest shapes — the Northern Cross — which makes it an easy constellation to learn.

## Mythology

The Greeks told several stories about this swan, and most of them involve Zeus in disguise.
In the best known, Zeus takes the form of a swan to approach Leda, queen of Sparta; from
that union come Helen of Troy and the twins of Gemini. The swan was placed in the sky to
commemorate the deception.

Another myth makes the swan a memorial of grief. Phaethon, son of the sun god, lost control
of his father's chariot and fell blazing into the river Eridanus. His friend Cygnus dived
again and again into the water searching for his body, until the gods took pity and
transformed him into a swan — and then set him among the stars, where he still glides along
the bright river of the Milky Way.

## Notable stars

- **Deneb** — the swan's tail, and the name says so: it comes from the Arabic for "tail."
  At magnitude 1.25 it looks like an ordinary bright star, but it lies some 2,600
  light-years away, far more distant than most stars of its brightness. To shine so
  clearly from that distance it must be one of the most luminous stars we can see.
- **Albireo** — the swan's beak, and the finest double star for a small telescope. Even
  modest optics split it into a golden-orange star and a smaller blue companion, a colour
  contrast that beginners rarely forget.
- **Sadr** — the heart of the swan, sitting where the neck meets the wings, about 1,500
  light-years away and buried in the glow of the Milky Way.
- **Al Fawaris** — "the riders," marking the tip of one wing. A hot blue-white star around
  170 light-years from us.
- **The Northern Cross** — Deneb, Sadr, Albireo and the two wingtip stars together, the
  long axis running down the Milky Way.

## How to find it

Look high overhead on a September evening from the Northern Hemisphere, near the zenith.
Deneb is the top of the cross and one corner of the Summer Triangle, together with Vega
and Altair — find that big triangle and Cygnus is the constellation attached to its
northernmost star.

From there, trace the long beam of the cross down and to the south until you reach Albireo
at the beak. Under a dark sky the swan flies directly along the Milky Way, and the band of
starlight splits in two beside it.
