## The Strongman

Hercules is the fifth-largest constellation in the sky, a sprawling kneeling figure that
fills the summer evenings between Lyra and Corona Borealis. Its stars are modest, but it
holds one of the finest sights in the northern sky — the great globular cluster M13.

## Mythology

Hercules — Heracles to the Greeks — was the son of Zeus and a mortal woman, and the
strongest of all heroes. To atone for a madness sent by Hera, in which he killed his own
family, he was set twelve labours: to strangle the Nemean lion, to kill the many-headed
Hydra, to capture the Cretan bull, to fetch the apples of the Hesperides, and more. Several
of his victims are nearby in the sky — Leo the lion, Hydra the water-snake, and Cancer the
crab that nipped his foot during the fight.

The Greeks knew this pattern of stars simply as the Kneeler, a nameless figure crouching
with one knee down, and only later attached Heracles to it. That older posture survives in
the constellation's shape: the figure hangs upside down in the northern sky, head toward
the south, with one foot raised above the horizon.

## Notable stars and objects

- **Kornephoros** — the brightest star in the constellation at magnitude 2.8, a yellow
  giant about 148 light-years away. Its name means "club-bearer."
- **Rasalgethi** — "the head of the kneeler," a deep red supergiant some 380 light-years
  off. At about 2750 K it is one of the coolest and reddest stars easily visible to the
  naked eye, and it varies slowly in brightness.
- **Zeta Herculis** — a close neighbour at just 35 light-years, and a binary whose two
  stars orbit each other in about 34 years.
- **Sarin** — the hero's shoulder, a hot blue-white star roughly 79 light-years away.
- **M13** — the Hercules Globular Cluster, a ball of several hundred thousand ancient
  stars. At magnitude 5.8 it is a faint smudge to the naked eye under dark skies, a fuzzy
  patch in binoculars, and a glittering swarm through a small telescope.

## How to find it

Look high in the south on a July evening from the Northern Hemisphere. Find Vega, the
brilliant blue-white star of Lyra, and then look to its west for a lopsided square of
four middling stars — the asterism known as the Keystone, which forms the hero's torso.
From its corners, two pairs of legs sprawl up toward Draco and two arms reach down toward
Ophiuchus.

The Keystone is also your signpost to M13: the cluster sits on the western edge of the
square, about a third of the way down from its top corner.
