## The Lesser Lion

Leo Minor is a small, faint scattering of stars squeezed between the Great Bear and the
Lion. Its brightest star is only magnitude 3.8, and it has no myth at all — it is one of
the sky's modern inventions, filling a quiet gap that the ancients left blank.

## History

The Greeks and Romans saw nothing here. The patch of sky between Ursa Major and Leo was
simply unformed — stars too dim to gather into a figure, left out of Ptolemy's catalogue of
forty-eight constellations.

It was the Polish astronomer Johannes Hevelius who gave the region a name, including Leo
Minor among the new constellations he introduced in 1687. The idea was plain enough: the
stars sat next to Leo, so he made them a smaller lion, a cub trailing beside the larger
beast. Hevelius invented several constellations this way, and seven of them, Leo Minor
among them, are still in official use today.

One small oddity survives from this history. When later astronomers assigned Greek letters
to the stars of Leo Minor, only one star received one, and it was Beta. The brightest
star, Praecipua, never got its Alpha, so Leo Minor is a constellation with a Beta but no
Alpha.

## Notable stars

- **Praecipua** — the brightest star in the constellation, at magnitude 3.8 and about 98
  light-years away. Its name simply means "the chief one" in Latin. It is an orange giant,
  cooler than the Sun at around 5000 K.
- **Beta Leonis Minoris** — magnitude 4.2 and some 146 light-years distant, another
  yellow-orange giant and the only star in the constellation with a Greek letter.
- **HR 3974** — magnitude 4.5 at about 91 light-years, and the hottest of the
  constellation's leading stars at roughly 8250 K, giving it a distinctly white cast.

## How to find it

Leo Minor is a spring constellation for the Northern Hemisphere, highest on April
evenings. Find Leo first — the bright Regulus and the backwards question mark of the Lion's
head — then look just above the Lion's back. The thin wedge of faint stars between Leo and
the Big Dipper's bowl is Leo Minor.

You will want a dark sky. Under city lights the Lesser Lion simply disappears.
