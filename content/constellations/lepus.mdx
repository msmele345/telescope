## The Hare

Lepus is a small, neat constellation crouching directly beneath the feet of Orion. It is
easy to overlook beside its brilliant neighbour, but its brightest stars form a tidy
figure of a hare pressed low to the ground.

## Mythology

The Greeks saw here a hare, placed in the sky as the quarry of Orion the Hunter — who
stands directly above it — or as the prey of his hunting dog, Canis Major, which follows
just to the east. In some tellings the hare is forever fleeing and never caught, a chase
frozen into the winter sky.

Another story explains how the hare got there in the first place. Hares were said to have
been unknown on the island of Leros until a young man brought in a pregnant female, after
which they bred so quickly that the whole island was overrun.

In another account the god Hermes placed the hare among the stars to honour its swiftness
and its fertility.

## Notable stars

- **Arneb** — the brightest star of the hare, marking its back. Its name comes from the
  Arabic for "the hare." At magnitude 2.6 it looks modest, but that is only distance at
  work: it lies roughly 1,280 light-years away, far beyond most of the stars around it.
- **Nihal** — the second brightest, at magnitude 2.8 and about 160 light-years away. A
  yellowish star whose name means "quenching their thirst," from an Arabic figure of
  camels drinking.
- **Epsilon Leporis** — a cooler, orange-tinted star of magnitude 3.2, some 227
  light-years off, marking the western end of the figure.
- **Gamma Leporis** — at 29 light-years, the closest of the group, and a wide double that
  binoculars split easily.

## How to find it

Find Orion first, then drop your gaze straight down from Rigel, his bright blue-white
foot. The small group of stars just below is Lepus. From the Southern Hemisphere, where
Orion rides high on summer evenings, the hare sits comfortably overhead.

January evenings are best. Sirius, the brightest star in the sky, lies to the east of
Lepus and makes a useful second signpost — the hare crouches between it and Rigel.
