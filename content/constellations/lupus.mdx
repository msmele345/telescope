## The Wolf

Lupus is a compact southern constellation wedged between Centaurus and Scorpius, made of
hot blue stars that almost all lie at much the same great distance from us. None of its
stars carries a traditional name and it has no bright deep-sky showpieces, but it is an
old figure with a strange history.

## Mythology

To the Greeks these stars were not a wolf at all but simply Therion, "the beast" — an
unnamed wild animal held by the neighbouring Centaur, who is usually shown carrying it
toward the Altar, Ara, as an offering to the gods. The creature had no story of its own;
it existed only as a prop in the Centaur's hands.

No particular animal was attached to it until a Latin translation of Ptolemy's work
identified the beast as a wolf, Lupus, and the name stuck. The figure may be older than
the Greeks: it probably descends from a Babylonian constellation, UR.IDIM, the "mad dog,"
a strange hybrid with the head and torso of a man and the legs and tail of a lion.

## Notable stars

- **Alpha Lupi** — the brightest star of the wolf at magnitude 2.3, a fiercely hot blue
  giant some 548 light-years away. It is a pulsating variable, though the changes are far
  too slight to notice by eye.
- **Beta Lupi** — magnitude 2.7 and about 524 light-years off, another blue star of the
  same family. Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, Epsilon and Eta all lie roughly 500 light-years
  away and were born together: Lupus is largely a single drifting association of young
  stars seen from the side.
- **Zeta Lupi** — the odd one out. At magnitude 3.4 it looks like the rest, but it is a
  cooler yellow star only 116 light-years away, a nearby interloper on the same line of
  sight.

## How to find it

Lupus is a Southern Hemisphere constellation, best placed on June evenings. Find Scorpius
and the red Antares, then look to the west — the stars of Lupus fill the gap between the
scorpion and the bright pair Alpha and Beta Centauri.

From mid-northern latitudes it skims the southern horizon, and the farther north you go,
the less of it rises. Travellers to the tropics or the south will find it an easy, if
quiet, catch.
