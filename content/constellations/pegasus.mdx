## The Winged Horse

Pegasus is one of the largest constellations in the sky, and in autumn its great body
rides high overhead as an enormous near-perfect square of stars. The horse is drawn
upside down, with his neck and head trailing off toward the southwest.

## Mythology

In Greek myth, Pegasus sprang from the blood of the Gorgon Medusa when Perseus cut off her
head — a white winged horse born fully grown from a monster's death. Wherever his hooves
struck the ground, springs burst forth; one of them, Hippocrene on Mount Helicon, became
sacred to the Muses as a source of poetic inspiration.

The hero Bellerophon tamed Pegasus with a golden bridle given by Athena and rode him into
battle against the fire-breathing Chimera. Flush with victory, Bellerophon tried to fly the
horse up to Olympus to join the gods. Zeus sent a gadfly to sting Pegasus; the horse
bucked, Bellerophon fell back to Earth, and Pegasus flew on alone to the stables of the
gods, where Zeus set him among the stars.

## Notable stars and objects

- **Enif** — the horse's muzzle and the constellation's brightest star, a cool orange
  supergiant about 670 light-years away. Its name is Arabic for "the nose."
- **Scheat** — a red giant at the northwest corner of the Square, roughly 200 light-years
  off and noticeably ruddy next to its blue-white companions.
- **Markab** — "the saddle," a hot blue-white star about 140 light-years away marking the
  southwest corner.
- **Algenib** — the southeast corner, a fiercely hot blue star some 330 light-years distant.
- **Helvetios** — a faint Sun-like star about 50 light-years away, too dim to be obvious to
  the eye but famous as the first ordinary star found to have a planet.
- **The Great Square** — Markab, Scheat and Algenib together with Alpheratz, which belongs
  to neighbouring Andromeda. The four form a huge, almost empty box.
- **M15** — a dense globular cluster near Enif, a fuzzy ball in binoculars and a swarm of
  stars in a small telescope.

## How to find it

Look high in the south on an October evening from the Northern Hemisphere. The Great Square
is the key: a large, conspicuously blank quadrilateral of four similar stars, tilted like a
baseball diamond. From its western side the horse's neck bends down to Enif.

From the Square's northeast corner, Alpheratz, two chains of stars run off to the east —
that is Andromeda, and following them leads you to the Andromeda Galaxy.
