## The Hero

Perseus is a bright, busy constellation riding high on winter evenings, set in the richest
part of the northern Milky Way. He holds the severed head of the Gorgon Medusa, marked by
one of the strangest stars in the sky.

## Mythology

Perseus was the son of Zeus and the mortal princess Danaë. Sent to fetch the head of
Medusa — a Gorgon whose gaze turned men to stone — he was armed by the gods with winged
sandals, a cap of invisibility and a mirror-bright shield. He found her asleep, watched her
reflection rather than her face, and struck.

Flying home with the head, he passed over the coast of Ethiopia and saw Andromeda chained
to a rock, left as a sacrifice to the sea monster Cetus. Perseus killed the monster, freed
her and married her. The whole cast was eventually placed in the sky together: Perseus
beside Andromeda, her parents Cepheus and Cassiopeia above them, and Cetus swimming away
to the south.

## Notable stars

- **Mirfak** — the constellation's brightest star at magnitude 1.8, a yellow-white
  supergiant about 590 light-years away. It sits in a loose scattering of fainter stars
  that binoculars resolve beautifully.
- **Algol** — the Gorgon's head, and the sky's most famous "winking" star. Every 2.9 days a
  dimmer companion passes in front of it and its brightness drops noticeably, from
  magnitude 2.1 to about 3.4 and back, over roughly ten hours. It lies just 93 light-years away.
- **Gorgonea Tertia** — "the third Gorgon," a cool red star of magnitude 3.4 near Algol
  that slowly varies in brightness of its own accord.
- **Epsilon Persei** — a fiercely hot blue star of magnitude 2.9, some 540 light-years
  off, marking the hero's trailing leg.
- **Menkib** — fainter at magnitude 4.0 and very distant, around 1,800 light-years, but one
  of the hottest naked-eye stars in Perseus.

## How to find it

Look almost overhead on a December evening from mid-northern latitudes. Find the
W of Cassiopeia first, then follow its stars down and to the east: the next bright star
you meet is Mirfak, with a curving chain of stars running out of it like a sweeping arc.

Algol lies a little to the south of Mirfak, towards Andromeda. It is worth checking on
more than one night — if it looks dimmer than you remember, you have caught the Gorgon
blinking.
