## The Southern Fish

Piscis Austrinus is a small, faint constellation with one brilliant exception: Fomalhaut,
a lone white star in an otherwise empty stretch of autumn sky. The fish is shown drinking
the stream of water poured out by Aquarius just above it.

## Mythology

The Greeks called this the Great Fish, and made it the parent of the two fishes of Pisces.
In one story the Syrian goddess Derceto fell into a lake near Bambyce and was carried
safely to shore by a great fish; in gratitude the fish was set among the stars, and its
offspring became the pair of fishes of the zodiac. The Greeks sometimes told the same tale
of Aphrodite, who leapt into a river to escape the monster Typhon and was rescued in fish
form.

The figure is older still. In Babylonian records these stars were simply "the Fish," linked
to Ea, the god of the waters, and the constellation was already drawn drinking from the
water jar of the figure we now call Aquarius. That arrangement of pourer and drinker has
survived more or less unchanged for three thousand years.

## Notable stars

- **Fomalhaut** — the name comes from the Arabic for "mouth of the whale." At magnitude
  1.16 it is one of the brightest stars in the sky, and at just 25 light-years away it is
  one of the nearest bright stars to the Sun. It is a hot white star, around 9250 K,
  surrounded by a famous ring of cold dust and debris.
- **Delta Piscis Austrini** — a yellowish star of magnitude 4.2, about 170 light-years
  away, close to Fomalhaut on the sky.
- **Beta Piscis Austrini** — magnitude 4.3 and about 148 light-years away, marking the
  body of the fish west of Fomalhaut.
- **Epsilon Piscis Austrini** — the most distant of the group at roughly 745 light-years,
  and the hottest, near 14000 K, though it shines at only magnitude 4.2.

## How to find it

Look south on an October evening — spring in the Southern Hemisphere, where the fish rides
high, and autumn in the north, where it skims low along the horizon. Fomalhaut is easy to
identify because nothing else nearby is anything like as bright; it is often called the
Lonely Star of Autumn.

To be sure of it, find the Great Square of Pegasus and drop a line from its western side
straight down toward the horizon. The first bright star you meet is Fomalhaut. The fainter
stars of the fish spread to its west in a flattened loop, best picked out from a dark site.
