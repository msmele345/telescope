## The Arrow

Sagitta is the third-smallest constellation in the sky, and one of the most literal: a
short, straight line of faint stars with a notch at one end, looking exactly like an arrow
in flight. It is small enough to hide inside the Summer Triangle, yet old enough to have
been catalogued by Ptolemy.

## Mythology

The Greeks could not agree whose arrow this was. In one telling it belongs to Heracles,
the shaft he loosed at the Stymphalian birds — or at the eagle that tormented Prometheus,
which is tidy, since the eagle Aquila flies just below the Arrow in the sky. In another it
is the arrow of Apollo, fired at the Cyclopes in revenge for forging the thunderbolt that
killed his son Asclepius.

A gentler version gives it to Eros, the dart that made Zeus fall in love. Whichever story
you prefer, the constellation is a weapon in mid-flight, frozen among the stars of the
summer Milky Way.

## Notable stars

- **Gamma Sagittae** — the arrowhead and the brightest star in the constellation at
  magnitude 3.5, a cool orange giant about 274 light-years away.
- **Delta Sagittae** — the middle of the shaft, magnitude 3.8 and cooler still, glowing a
  deep orange from some 448 light-years off.
- **Sham** — Alpha Sagittae, one of the two stars forming the feathered notch at the back
  of the arrow. Its name comes from the Arabic for "arrow." Magnitude 4.4, about 473
  light-years away.
- **Beta Sagittae** — Sham's partner at the tail, a near twin in brightness at magnitude
  4.4 and at almost the same distance.

## How to find it

Look high overhead on a September evening in the Northern Hemisphere, inside the great
Summer Triangle of Vega, Deneb and Altair. Sagitta lies just north of Altair, pointing
roughly eastward between the bright star and the long rift of the Milky Way.

None of its stars are bright, so wait for a dark night and let your eyes adjust. Once you
catch the little arrow, you will never lose it again — the shape is too neat to mistake.
