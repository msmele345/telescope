## The Sculptor's Studio

Sculptor is a small, dim patch of southern sky with no star brighter than fourth magnitude.
It commemorates not a hero or a beast but a workshop — the studio of a sculptor, complete
with block of marble and tools.

## History

Sculptor is one of fourteen constellations invented by the French astronomer Nicolas-Louis
de Lacaille, who catalogued the southern sky from the Cape of Good Hope in 1751 and 1752.
Where the Greeks had filled their sky with gods and monsters, Lacaille filled the southern
gaps with the instruments of the Enlightenment: a telescope, an air pump, a microscope, a
painter's easel — and this one, which he named Apparatus Sculptoris, "the sculptor's
studio."

Later astronomers found the name unwieldy and trimmed it to Sculptor. There is no myth
attached to these stars, and no ancient figure beneath them; the constellation is simply a
tribute to a craft, drawn across a region too faint to have attracted a story of its own.

## Notable stars

- **Alpha Sculptoris** — the brightest star in the constellation at magnitude 4.3, though
  only just. It is a hot blue-white giant about 673 light-years away, with a surface near
  16,000 K.
- **Beta Sculptoris** — magnitude 4.4 and much closer, about 178 light-years off, another
  blue-white star.
- **Gamma Sculptoris** — nearly identical in brightness to Beta and at a similar distance
  of about 179 light-years, but cooler and orange in colour.
- **Eta Sculptoris** — fainter at magnitude 4.8 and some 548 light-years away. At around
  2,900 K it is one of the coolest stars here, a deep red that binoculars bring out well.

## How to find it

Sculptor sits in the Southern Hemisphere's spring sky — autumn for northern observers —
and is best placed on November evenings. It is easiest to locate by its neighbours: find
bright Fomalhaut, the solitary star of Piscis Austrinus, then look south and east toward
Achernar at the end of Eridanus. Sculptor fills the dark space between them, tucked
beneath the tail of Cetus.

Do not expect a shape to leap out. Under a dark sky you will see a scattering of faint
stars, and that is all the sculptor's studio ever was.
