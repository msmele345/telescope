## The Shield

Scutum is a small, faint constellation squeezed between Aquila and Sagittarius, with no
star brighter than fourth magnitude. What makes it worth a visit is its setting: it sits
against one of the richest stretches of the Milky Way, and it holds one of the finest open
clusters in the sky.

## History

Scutum has no ancient myth behind it. It was invented in 1684 by the Polish astronomer
Johannes Hevelius, who called it Scutum Sobiescianum — the Shield of Sobieski — in honour
of King John III Sobieski of Poland, whose forces had helped turn back the Ottoman siege
of Vienna the year before. It is one of the very few constellations named for a real
historical person rather than a god or a beast.

Later astronomers trimmed the king's name away, and the figure survives on modern maps
simply as Scutum, the Shield. Hevelius drew several constellations in this part of the
sky; this one and a handful of others were kept when the official list was fixed in the
twentieth century.

## Notable stars and objects

- **Alpha Scuti** — the brightest star of the shield at magnitude 3.9, an orange giant
  about 174 light-years away.
- **Beta Scuti** — magnitude 4.2 and the most distant of the group at roughly 690
  light-years, a yellow giant far more luminous than it looks.
- **Delta Scuti** — magnitude 4.7, about 187 light-years off. It pulses slightly in
  brightness every few hours, and has given its name to a whole class of variable stars.
- **The Wild Duck Cluster (M11)** — a dense open cluster of magnitude 6.3, visible as a
  hazy patch in binoculars and resolving into hundreds of stars in a small telescope. Its
  V-shaped scatter of brighter members reminded early observers of a flight of ducks.

## How to find it

Look on an August evening, when Scutum stands reasonably high for observers in both
hemispheres. Start from Altair, the bright star at the head of Aquila the Eagle, and follow
the eagle's body southwest; Scutum's modest quadrilateral of stars lies just beyond, on the
edge of the Milky Way.

Do not expect a striking pattern. Scutum rewards patience and a pair of binoculars — sweep
the area slowly and you will come across M11 and the glowing star clouds behind it.
