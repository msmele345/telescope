## The Bull

Taurus is one of the most striking constellations in the sky — a fierce bull's head with
glowing red eyes, charging westward across the winter heavens.

## Mythology

In Greek myth, Taurus is Zeus himself in disguise. Smitten with the Phoenician princess
Europa, Zeus transformed into a magnificent white bull and walked among her father's herds.
When Europa, charmed by the gentle bull, climbed onto its back, Zeus charged into the sea
and carried her across to Crete, where she became the mother of King Minos.

Older still are the Egyptian and Mesopotamian bull cults — Taurus marked the spring
equinox around 4000 BCE, the start of the agricultural year, and was sacred wherever
cattle were central to civilization. The Bull of Heaven that Gilgamesh fights in the
ancient Mesopotamian epic is almost certainly Taurus.

## Notable stars and objects

- **Aldebaran** — the bull's red eye. An aging orange giant about 65 light-years away,
  Aldebaran is the 14th brightest star in the sky and the easiest way to confirm you are
  looking at Taurus.
- **The Pleiades** (M45) — also called the Seven Sisters, this cluster of young blue stars
  rides on the bull's shoulder. The naked eye can resolve six or seven; binoculars reveal
  hundreds.
- **The Hyades** — the V-shaped cluster forming the bull's face. The closest open cluster
  to Earth, only about 150 light-years away.
- **The Crab Nebula** (M1) — the wreckage of a supernova whose explosion was witnessed and
  recorded by Chinese astronomers in 1054 CE.

## How to find it

Find Orion's belt, then follow it up and to the right (in the Northern Hemisphere). The
first bright orange star you hit is Aldebaran. From there the V of the Hyades opens to the
left, and the Pleiades cluster glitters further up and to the right.
