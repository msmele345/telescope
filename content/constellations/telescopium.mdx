## The Telescope

Telescopium is a small, faint constellation tucked into the far southern sky, below
Sagittarius and Corona Australis. It has no bright stars, no myths and no deep-sky showpieces — it is
one of the sky's quiet corners, named for the instrument that revealed everything else.

## History

Telescopium is a modern invention. The French astronomer Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille mapped
the southern sky from the Cape of Good Hope in the 1750s and filled its gaps with fourteen
new constellations, nearly all of them named for the tools of science and the arts: the
clock, the compass, the air pump, the sculptor's studio. Telescopium honours the aerial
telescope, the long, unwieldy refractor of the seventeenth century.

Lacaille drew the figure rather larger than it is today, stretching north between
Sagittarius and Scorpius. In the nineteenth century Francis Baily and Benjamin Gould
handed several of its stars to neighbouring constellations, leaving a modest patch of
faint points — but the name stayed, as Lacaille's names generally have.

## Notable stars

- **Alpha Telescopii** — the brightest star in the constellation at magnitude 3.5, about
  250 light-years away. It is a hot blue subgiant with a surface near 24,000 K, far
  fiercer than our Sun.
- **Zeta Telescopii** — the second brightest at magnitude 4.1, and the nearest of the
  group at about 127 light-years. An orange giant of roughly 5,200 K, a little cooler
  than the Sun but far larger.
- **Xi Telescopii** — a cool red giant around 3,350 K, glowing at magnitude 4.9 from some
  1,250 light-years away. The most distant of its brightest stars.

## How to find it

Telescopium strongly favours the Southern Hemisphere, where it rides high on winter
evenings around August. The whole figure is visible only south of about 33°N; from the Mediterranean
or the southern United States just its upper edge barely clears the horizon.

Start at Scorpius and find the bright red Antares, then follow the scorpion's curving tail
to its stinger. Telescopium lies to the southeast, below the little arc of Corona Australis
and between the stinger and the brilliant star Alpha Pavonis. Expect a faint, unremarkable scatter of stars — finding it at
all is the achievement, and a dark sky is essential.
