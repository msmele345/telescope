## The Southern Triangle

Triangulum Australe is a small constellation, but an unusually tidy one: three bright
stars forming a compact, almost equilateral triangle. It is brighter and easier to pick
out than its northern namesake, Triangulum.

## History

The southern triangle has no classical myth — it lies too far south to have been visible
from Greece or Babylon. European astronomers learned of it from the voyages of the late
sixteenth century, and it appeared on a celestial globe published by the Dutch
cartographer Petrus Plancius in 1589, drawn from reports brought back by navigators
sailing the southern oceans.

Johann Bayer drew it more accurately in his great star atlas *Uranometria* of 1603, which
gave it its current name, and Lacaille assigned the Greek-letter labels still used today in
1756. The constellation's three brightest stars carry the names Atria, Betria and Gatria —
simply their Bayer designations, alpha, beta and gamma, folded into the constellation's
name. Atria is an official IAU name; Betria and Gatria are informal, and among the
youngest star names in the sky.

## Notable stars

- **Atria** — the brightest corner of the triangle at magnitude 1.9, an orange giant about
  420 light-years away. Its cooler surface, around 4700 K, gives it a distinctly warm tint
  next to its two white companions.
- **Betria** — magnitude 2.9 and only about 40 light-years from us, making it by far the
  nearest of the three.
- **Gatria** — magnitude 2.9 as well, but a hot white star some 183 light-years away.
  Together with Betria it closes the triangle.

## How to find it

Triangulum Australe is a Southern Hemisphere constellation, riding high on winter evenings
around July. Start from Alpha and Beta Centauri, the two brilliant pointers beside the
Southern Cross, and continue along that line away from the Cross. You will cross the faint
stars of Circinus and run straight into the triangle.

The figure is small — a hand's breadth at most — but its three bright corners form such a
tidy triangle, with orange Atria at the apex, that once you have seen it, you will never
mistake it for anything else.
