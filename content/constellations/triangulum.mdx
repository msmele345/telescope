## The Triangle

Triangulum is exactly what its name says: three modest stars making a narrow, long-limbed
triangle between Andromeda and Aries. It is small and quiet, but it holds one of the great
prizes of the northern sky — the Triangulum Galaxy.

## Mythology

The Greeks called this little figure Deltoton, after the capital letter delta, and that
shape carried several associations. Eratosthenes linked it with the delta of the Nile, the
fan of river mouths that made Egypt fertile. The Roman writer Hyginus saw instead the
island of Sicily, known as Trinacria for its three corners; Ceres, the patron goddess of
Sicily, was said to have begged Jupiter to set the island in the heavens.

For a constellation this faint, no hero or monster ever quite stuck — it remained a shape
rather than a story.

## Notable stars and objects

- **Beta Trianguli** — the brightest of the three, a white star about 124 light-years away
  at magnitude 3.0. With Gamma it forms the short base of the triangle.
- **Mothallah** — Alpha Trianguli, whose Arabic name means "the triangle." Slightly
  fainter than Beta at magnitude 3.4, but much closer to us, about 64 light-years away.
  It sits at the sharp apex, pointing south toward Aries.
- **Gamma Trianguli** — the other end of the base, a hot blue-white star of magnitude 4.0
  about 118 light-years from Earth.
- **The Triangulum Galaxy** — M33, a spiral galaxy and the third-largest member of our
  Local Group after Andromeda and the Milky Way. At magnitude 5.7 it is just within reach
  of sharp eyes under a truly dark sky, which makes it one of the most distant objects
  anyone has seen without a telescope. Binoculars show it as a large, soft oval glow.

## How to find it

Look high in the south on a December evening from the Northern Hemisphere. Find the
Andromeda chain of stars running out from the corner of the Great Square of Pegasus, then
drop a little south and east: the thin triangle lies between Andromeda and the flat little
hook of Aries, about halfway from Almach, the last star of the chain, to Hamal in Aries.

Once you have the triangle, sweep the space between Mothallah and Andromeda's Mirach with
binoculars — M33 hides there, faint and wide and easy to pass over if you look too hard.
