## The Little Bear

Ursa Minor is a smaller, fainter version of its bigger neighbor — the Little Dipper. Its
most important star is Polaris, the North Star, which sits almost exactly above Earth's
north pole and barely moves all night.

## Mythology

In Greek myth, Ursa Minor is Arcas, the son of the nymph Callisto. After Hera transformed
Callisto into a bear, Arcas grew up not knowing his mother. When he encountered her in the
woods as a hunter, Zeus intervened to prevent the killing — placing them both in the sky
as the Great Bear and the Little Bear.

In other traditions, Ursa Minor is a wing of a much larger figure, or a dog, or a chariot.
But in nearly every navigational tradition the constellation is special for one reason
alone: Polaris.

## Notable stars

- **Polaris** — the North Star sits at the end of the Little Dipper's handle. It marks
  true north to within about half a degree, and unlike every other star, it stays still
  while the rest of the sky wheels around it. For thousands of years sailors used it to
  steer.
- **Kochab and Pherkad** — the two outer stars of the Dipper's bowl, sometimes called
  "the Guardians of the Pole" because they slowly orbit Polaris over the course of the
  night.

## How to find it

Find the Big Dipper first. Trace a line from Merak through Dubhe — the two outer stars of
the Big Dipper's bowl — and extend it about five times the distance between them. You will
land on Polaris. From Polaris, the rest of the Little Dipper curves down and back, with
its bowl ending at Kochab and Pherkad.

Polaris is not particularly bright — about the same brightness as the stars in the Big
Dipper. Its fame comes from its position, not its brilliance.
