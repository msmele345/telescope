## The Sails

Vela is the billowing sail of a ship — one third of a vast constellation that once filled the
southern sky. It has no Messier objects and no stars with familiar names, but it carries some
of the hottest, strangest suns visible to the unaided eye.

## Mythology

Vela was not originally a constellation of its own. It was part of Argo Navis, the great ship
of Jason and the Argonauts, who sailed to Colchis to win the Golden Fleece. The Argo was built
with a speaking timber from the oracle at Dodona, carried a crew of heroes including Heracles
and Orpheus, and survived the clashing rocks of the Bosphorus. When the voyage was over, the
ship was dedicated to Poseidon and set among the stars.

Argo Navis was so enormous that in the eighteenth century the French astronomer Nicolas-Louis
de Lacaille broke it into three workable pieces: Carina the keel, Puppis the stern, and Vela
the sails. They are still arranged as the ship was, and a stargazer who knows all three can
reassemble the Argo in the southern sky.

## Notable stars

- **Gamma² Velorum** — the brightest star in Vela at magnitude 1.8, about 841 light-years
  away. It is a multiple system containing the nearest Wolf-Rayet star to Earth, a stripped
  stellar core blazing at enormous temperature and shedding its outer layers into space.
  Sometimes called the "Spectral Gem of the Southern Skies."
- **Delta Velorum** — magnitude 2.0 and only about 80 light-years away, making it one of the
  closer bright stars in the sky. It is an eclipsing binary whose brightness dips slightly
  when one component passes in front of the other.
- **Suhail** — the orange giant Lambda Velorum, magnitude 2.2, roughly 573 light-years off. At
  about 4,400 K it is distinctly cooler and ruddier than its blue-white neighbours.
- **Kappa Velorum** — magnitude 2.5 and some 539 light-years away, a fiercely hot blue star of
  around 26,000 K.

## How to find it

Vela is a southern constellation, best placed on March evenings, high overhead from the
Southern Hemisphere and barely clearing the horizon from the mid-northern latitudes.

The easiest way in is the False Cross, a kite-shaped figure made from Delta and Kappa Velorum
together with two stars of neighbouring Carina. It is larger and less tilted than the true
Southern Cross, and has misled more than one navigator. From the False Cross, work west and
north along the Milky Way to find Suhail and the brilliant Gamma² Velorum.
