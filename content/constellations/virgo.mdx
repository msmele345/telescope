## The Maiden

Virgo is the second-largest constellation in the sky, a sprawling figure of a woman
holding a sheaf of wheat. Despite its size, only one star in it is truly bright: Spica.

## Mythology

Virgo has been associated with goddesses of harvest and justice across many cultures. In
Greek myth she is most often Demeter, the goddess of grain, mourning her daughter
Persephone's annual descent into the underworld — Demeter's grief is why crops fail and
winter comes. The sheaf of wheat she holds in the constellation, marked by the bright
star Spica, reflects her dominion over the harvest.

In other traditions she is Astraea, the virgin goddess of justice, the last of the gods
to leave Earth at the end of the Golden Age. As humans grew wicked, she withdrew to the
heavens, where she now holds the scales of justice — the neighboring constellation Libra.

## Notable stars

- **Spica** — the "ear of wheat," and Virgo's only first-magnitude star. A blue-white
  binary about 250 light-years away. Its two component stars orbit so closely they have
  distorted each other into egg shapes.
- **Porrima** — a famous binary star whose two components orbit each other every 169
  years. Its name comes from a Roman goddess of childbirth.

## How to find it

Use the Big Dipper. Follow the curve of its handle outward — it leads first to the bright
orange Arcturus (in Boötes), and continuing on the same arc, to Spica. Astronomers call
this trick **"arc to Arcturus, speed on to Spica."** Once you have Spica, the body of
Virgo spreads out around it.

Virgo is best viewed in spring and early summer, when the Sun is far from it. In late
summer and autumn the Sun passes through the constellation.
