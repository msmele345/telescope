## The Little Fox

Vulpecula is a small, faint constellation tucked inside the Summer Triangle, with no star
brighter than fourth magnitude. It rewards patience rather than a glance — and it holds one
of the finest planetary nebulae in the sky.

## History

Vulpecula has no ancient myth. It was invented by the Polish astronomer Johannes Hevelius
in the late seventeenth century, who filled several gaps in the northern sky with new
figures drawn from the natural world. He called this one *Vulpecula cum Ansere* — "the
little fox with the goose" — and drew a fox carrying a goose in its jaws. He placed it among
other hunting animals, near Aquila the eagle and the vulture (an old reading of Lyra), and
explained that the fox was carrying the goose off to Cerberus, another of his inventions
that did not last.

Later astronomers trimmed the name to Vulpecula and the goose quietly vanished, but a trace
of it survives in the name of the constellation's brightest star, Lucida Anseris — "the
brightest of the goose."

## Notable stars and objects

- **Lucida Anseris** — Alpha Vulpeculae, the constellation's leading light at magnitude
  4.4, about 300 light-years away. It is a cool red giant, and its colour is just
  detectable in binoculars.
- **HR 7744** — a second orange giant of magnitude 4.5, roughly 330 light-years distant,
  and barely fainter than Alpha.
- **HR 7306** — the most distant of the constellation's brighter stars at about 900
  light-years. At magnitude 4.8 it looks modest, but it is a fiercely hot blue star whose
  surface runs near 22,000 K.
- **The Dumbbell Nebula** — M27, a planetary nebula of magnitude 7.5: the expanding shell
  of gas thrown off by a dying star. It is one of the easiest nebulae for a small
  telescope, showing a plump two-lobed shape that earned it its name.

## How to find it

From the Northern Hemisphere, look high in the south on a September evening. Find the
Summer Triangle — Vega, Deneb and Altair — and then the little arrow of Sagitta
below Albireo, the star at the swan's head. Vulpecula is the dim scatter of stars between Sagitta
and Cygnus.

For M27, drop south from Albireo to Sagitta, then swing a little east: the nebula lies just
above the arrow's tip. From a dark site, binoculars show it as a small grey puff, no bigger than a star bloated
out of focus.
